Timing matters. US markets close after India's. The Dow, Nasdaq and S&P figures above are 3 September closes — they were the cue for today's Indian open, and tonight's US session is the cue for Monday. They never explain the session being reported. Brent, gold, silver and the rupee trade through Indian hours and did move today.

The transmission chain, named. Today reverses yesterday's chain almost exactly. Yesterday oil rose and fear rose with it; today one Federal Reserve official said inflation looked to be improving, US rate-hike odds fell, Wall Street had its best day in a month, and the effect travelled into India along three paths. First, into the open: the Sensex gapped up and snapped a four-day losing streak, and Nifty September futures moved from a short buildup to short covering. Second, into the currency: softer US rate expectations weakened the dollar, emerging-market currencies firmed, and the rupee held near a two-month high — which, combined with record reserves, is why India could absorb $95 oil without strain. Third, into safe havens, in reverse: gold fell 1.54% and silver 1.60% on MCX, unwinding yesterday's geopolitical bid. Where the chain did not reach is the domestic economy — 264 shares still fell, FMCG still has zero constituents above their 20-day average, and Auto breadth is down 46 points in five sessions. A friendlier global backdrop lifted the index; it did not fix what is wrong beneath it. And one purely domestic event, UltraTech's entry into wires and cables, overwhelmed all of it for an entire industry.

A/D D-1 is the advance/decline for yesterday. %>20D D-1 is the percentage of stocks in that index trading above their 20-day SMA, yesterday.

A/D Today tells you how the index performed today — what the advance-to-decline ratio was. %>20D SMA tells you what percentage of stocks in that universe is trading above its 20-day simple moving average. Watch how the A/D ratio moved from last week to today, and how %>20D SMA moved over the same stretch — those two shaded pairs are the comparison this table exists for.

A rising index with increasing advances and more stocks above the 20D SMA means broader participation in the rally. A rising index on very narrow participation can instead mean index management, indecision, or only a few sectors doing the work while the rest of the market goes nowhere.

Eight sectors moved enough in breadth to flag. They split into a banking-and-energy group that is quietly strengthening and a manufacturing-and-defensive group that is deteriorating fast.

NIFTY IT — the worst breadth for a second consecutive day: 0.25, meaning four constituents fell for every one that rose, and the share above the 20-day average has now dropped 40 points in three sessions to 30%. The sector index fell 0.47% and is down 1.88% over the week. This is the same structural story — Nifty IT is down roughly 29% in 2026 on fears that AI tooling erodes the traditional services model — and the bounce we flagged yesterday has now been fully surrendered.

NIFTY HEALTHCARE — breadth of 0.18, worse than IT: roughly five constituents fell for every one that rose. The share above the 20-day average is 25% after a 20-point drop in three sessions, and the sector index fell 0.87%, the worst of any sector today. Over one week it is down 2.32%. Pharma fell 0.68% alongside it and has lost 15 points of breadth. Two sectors that were 7th and 4th ranked a month ago are now unwinding together, with no news attached — the signature of a crowded position being exited.

NIFTY PSU BANK — the share above the 20-day average rose 25 points in three sessions to 50%, one of the largest improvements on the board. But note the tension: today's advance/decline was only 0.83, and the index fell 0.44%. Breadth improving over three sessions while price falls today is a normal, healthy pause, not a reversal.

NIFTY CAPITAL GOODS — the day's most dramatic reversal. Yesterday 3.17 constituents rose for every one that fell; today 0.61. It still supplied 9 of the 40 scanner names from a 50-stock index, 2.25 times its fair share — but where yesterday 8 of 10 were gainers, today 6 of 9 are losers: KEI, Polycab, Finolex Cables, Kirloskar Oil Engines, Thermax and Jyoti CNC, against gains in Carborundum Universal, HBL Engineering and APL Apollo. Finolex Cables and Jyoti CNC were gainers in this same table yesterday. The UltraTech announcement is doing most of this work.

NIFTY REALTY — a sharp giveback. Yesterday's best sector, up 2.58%, fell 0.90% today with an advance/decline of 0.25 — four constituents down for every one up. Brigade, yesterday's 11.3% star, dropped 2.27% on above-average volume. The five-day breadth trend is still positive at +10 points and the sector remains 2nd over one month and up 18.74% over three, so this reads as profit-taking after a vertical day rather than a change of direction.

NIFTY AUTO — the most damaged sector in the market. An advance/decline of 0.15 means roughly seven constituents fell for every one that rose. Just 7% are above their 20-day average — one stock in fifteen. Breadth is down 46 points over five sessions, by far the worst on the board, and the index has fallen 3.95% in a week and 4.58% in a month. This is now deterioration on every measure at once.

NIFTY METAL — the one clear bright spot. 2.75 constituents rose for every one that fell, the index gained 1.07% — the best sector of the day — and 53% now sit above their 20-day average. This resolves the contradiction we flagged yesterday, when every MCX metal rose but metal shares lost 27 points of breadth. Today the shares followed, even though the metals themselves fell. APL Apollo was the sector's scanner name.

NIFTY FMCG — a second consecutive day with zero of fifteen constituents above their 20-day average. The advance/decline was 0.36 and the index fell 0.14%, and it remains the worst sector over one month at −7.34% and over three months at −4.82%. Two days at absolute zero breadth is no longer a single bad session; it is a sector where not one constituent can hold a short-term average.

The rotation has sharpened into something simpler than yesterday: money is moving into banks and energy, and out of manufacturing and defensives. All three banking indices and oil and gas are in the top five for breadth gains; auto, consumer durables, healthcare and IT are all in the bottom five. First, Nifty Next 50 losing 26 points of breadth is not a sector call — it is a size call, and it says the selling is broad among mid-large names. Two facts sit awkwardly together today. Domestic institutions made their largest single-day purchase in our entire record — ₹8,930 crore — and the Nifty went up 24 points while 264 shares fell. Money that size normally moves an index further than that. When it does not, it is because someone is selling into it, and the foreign sale of ₹3,112 crore only accounts for part of the other side. The global backdrop genuinely improved: rate-hike fears eased, Wall Street had its best day in a month, the rupee held near a two-month high on record reserves, and gold and silver gave back their fear premium. That is what lifted the index. What it did not lift is the domestic economy's own sectors — auto at 7% breadth, FMCG at zero for a second day, healthcare and IT unwinding in tandem. And the day's loudest event was neither global nor macro: one cement company announced it was entering the wires business, and an entire industry lost up to 9%. That is what a market with no margin for surprise looks like. The option chain has narrowed to a 100-point box between 23,900 and 24,000, and India VIX has fallen to 10.62. GUIDE & METHODOLOGY

How to Read This Report

The order matters more than the sections. Most readers open at the index level and stop there — which is the habit that makes a narrow market look healthy.
Step 1

Start with participation, not the index

Open §7. Which direction is the number of stocks above their 20-day average moving? That is the market’s direction of travel, not the index close.

Step 2

Then check the index against it

If the Nifty rose but fewer stocks are above their 20-day average than yesterday, participation is falling inside a rising index. That divergence is the most important thing on the page.

Step 3

Read sector leadership across timeframes

In §6b, compare each sector’s 1-day move against its 1-week and 1-month bars. A sector strong on the day but weak on the month is a bounce. A sector strong on all three is a trend.

Step 4

Cross-check each sector&rsquo;s move against its own breadth

A sector that rose today but whose participation column is falling has a narrow move — a few stocks doing the work. §6b row next to §7 row is the comparison.

Step 5

Go straight to the flagged sectors

The pack has already joined breadth to the scanners for you. Read §7’s notable flags and §9’s scanner names together — that join is already done.

Step 6

Ask whether the money agrees with the positioning

Compare §11 (what institutions bought and sold) with §13 (where the derivatives positioning sits). Conviction shows when both point the same way.
