What's Unusual Today

  • BRIGADE — 11.3% on 6.43× volume · RSI 75 · 13.7% above 20SMA
    The largest move in the Nifty 500 today. Brigade rode a sector-wide realty rally but outperformed it by roughly 2x. The volume spike follows a September 1 investor dialogue. At RSI 75 and 13.7% above 20SMA, the move is extended — consolidation before the next leg is more likely than immediate continuation.
  • ANANTRAJ — 6.7% on 8.81× volume · RSI 59 · 2.2% above 20SMA
    Anant Raj is the more interesting of the two realty movers: volume at 8.81× the 63-day average is extreme, yet the stock is only 2.2% above its 20-day average — there was room to run technically. FY26 revenue was ₹25.1 billion (+22%) and PAT ₹5.57 billion (+31%). The data centre segment is a differentiated angle in a market where most realty names are pure residential. Analyst target ~₹710 vs today's close of ₹628; strong institutional buying confirmed.
  • ELECON — 4.64% on 11.52× volume · RSI 50 · order book ₹1,518 Cr
    The highest volume multiple of the session at 11.52×. The trigger: a scheduled investor/analyst meeting in Mumbai today. Elecon's order book grew 37% year-on-year to ₹1,518 crore as of June 30. Management targets 22–24% EBITDA margins in MHE and 24% in Gears. RSI at 49.9 and 0.8% above 20SMA means the stock entered this move without baggage — a clean entry signal if the meeting delivers positive updates.
  • FII net −₹2,346 Cr vs DII net +₹4,977 Cr — absorption 212% 7 of 38
    FIIs ranked 7th of 38 sessions for selling — heavy. DIIs ranked 33rd of 38 for buying — very heavy. DII absorbed 212% of FII selling. Despite this, the Nifty fell 41 points — meaning the selling was in liquid, index-heavy names and the buying was in breadth names. That is not a contradiction; it is how the index can fall while 60% of stocks rise.
  • FIRSTCRY — 4.6% on 8.33× volume · RSI 28.5 · −9.3% below 20SMA
    An oversold snap-back, not a fundamental event. RSI of 28.5 before the move and −9.3% below 20SMA tell you how steep the prior decline was. Volume at 8.33× suggests short-covering or value buying, not institutional accumulation. No specific dated catalyst found. Stock remains well below its moving average — one day of volume does not change that.
  • JYOTICNC — 7.45% on 2.58× volume · ADX 38 · capacity expansion milestone
    Jyoti CNC hit an intraday high of ₹1,039. The move is trend-confirming rather than trend-initiating: ADX 38.2, RSI 66.8, 10.6% above 20SMA. The catalyst — 16,000-machine annual capacity going live this month — had been known; the market is now pricing it in. Order book: ₹4,732–4,848 crore across aerospace, defence and automotive. PTC Industries investor meetings on Sep 4–7 may keep adjacent defence/capital goods names in play.
  • RBLBANK — 4.8% on 6× volume · 52-week high ₹409 · FCNR-B disclosure
    The clearest, most actionable catalyst of the day: RBL Bank disclosed it mobilised ₹32,472 crore ($3.4 billion) in FCNR(B) deposits under the RBI's special forex swap facility. The RBI faces an estimated $10.6–12.7 billion cost on these swaps over five years — that cost is the RBI's, not RBL's. For the bank, FCNR-B deposits are cheap, stable foreign-currency liabilities. RSI at 70.4 is stretched but ADX at 17.8 says the trend has not established — further upside needs a fresh catalyst.
  • NIFTY 50 ATR — 0.76% of price · 62nd percentile of 250 sessions 62 of 250
    Nifty is calmer than 62% of the last 250 trading days. An ATR of 0.76% means the average daily range is under 185 points. The market does not stay this compressed when the index is 3% below its 200-day average. Low volatility at a technically weak level is a setup — the direction of resolution is what the bulls and bears are fighting about.
  • NIFTY 500 A/D — 1.51 · 72nd percentile of 246 sessions 177 of 246
    A breadth reading in the top 28% of sessions on a day the headline index fell. This is the central statistical fact of September 3. The Nifty 50 went down; the Nifty 500 did not. Large-cap selling with broad-market buying is rotation, not capitulation. The GST August data released today (₹1.99 lakh crore, +14.8% YoY) likely helped mid and small cap sentiment independently of index direction.

    The fastest acceleration of participation in the private bank sector in the recent data window. Bank Nifty +209 points on a day the Nifty fell confirms the number is real. The next two sessions will indicate which.

Macro &amp; Policy Dashboard

GST collections — ₹1.99 lakh crore in August, +14.8% YoY Strong

Released Sep 1, 2026 · Ministry of Finance
  • August gross GST: ₹1,99,853 crore — the third straight month above ₹1.9 lakh crore.
  • Import-related GST surged 29% YoY; domestic transactions grew 9.3%.
  • Net collections (post refunds) grew 8.3% to ₹1.68 lakh crore — refunds rose 68%, flagged for GST Council review.
  • Strong collections reduce fiscal slippage risk and support sentiment for rate-sensitive sectors.

SEBI proposes MF net settlement in cash market Regulatory

Consultation paper released Sep 3, 2026 · Comments due Sep 24
  • SEBI proposes mutual funds may net fund flows (buy vs sell) within the same settlement cycle in the cash market.
  • Securities settlement stays gross — delivery-backed, unchanged.
  • Goal: reduce temporary liquidity pressure during high-churn periods (index rebalancing, large redemptions).
  • If adopted, this reduces intraday volume spikes during rebalancing events — a structural change to how peak-day volumes are interpreted.

NSE IPO — price band expected Sep 11, listing Sep 25 Event

Sources · Sep 3–4, 2026
  • NSE is expected to announce its price band around September 11, with listing targeted for September 25.
  • 100% OFS structure: ~14.89 crore shares (~6% of paid-up capital). No fresh issue.
  • Indicative valuation: ₹5.2 lakh crore — one of the largest Indian IPOs ever, potentially rivalling LIC.
  • NSE equity listing will create a new benchmark position — ETFs and index-linked funds will be forced buyers. Expect pre-listing momentum in brokerage and fintech adjacents.

RBI holds repo at 5.25% — FCNR swap cost estimated at $10.6–12.7 bn Neutral

MPC Aug 3–5 · Next meeting Oct 5–7
  • Repo unchanged at 5.25%, neutral stance. No rate action until at least October.
  • Q1 FY27 GDP: 7.8% — above RBI's 7.0% estimate. Full-year revised to 6.7%.
  • CPI 4.45% in July — within tolerance band but trending up. Food and energy remain the swing factors.
  • New data point: RBI's special FCNR-B swap facility will cost the central bank an estimated $10.6–12.7 billion over five years. This is a sovereign cost, not a bank cost — but it signals the rupee stabilisation effort was expensive.

IIP at 6.7% in July 2026 — manufacturing leads Positive

Released Aug 2026 · Ministry of Statistics
  • Industrial production grew 6.7% in July, driven by manufacturing (+7.3%) and electricity (+8.7%).
  • Mining contracted (−0.9%) — a segment to watch given Metal sector outperformance on the month.
  • Strong IIP + strong GDP + strong GST is a three-way confirmation of domestic demand. The market's underperformance relative to fundamentals is a global risk premium story, not a domestic growth concern.

Sector Rotation

Rotating in

  • NIFTY PVT BANK — +20 points of participation over five sessions, ranked 6th of 14 on the month. Today's +209 point gain in Bank Nifty confirmed the number. FCNR-B deposit story (RBL Bank, ₹32,472 crore) adds a sector-level catalyst — banks with dollar liability books benefit from the RBI's swap facility.
  • NIFTY OIL & GAS — +13 points of participation, ranked 11th on the month. Brent at $95.63 helps upstream names; downstream refiners still carry the margin risk. Rotation in on a relative basis despite difficult absolute positioning.
  • NIFTY REALTY — +10 points of participation, ranked 3rd on the month. BRIGADE +11.3% and ANANTRAJ +6.7% were the two largest stock moves in the Nifty 500. Brigade's ₹9,000 crore FY27 pre-sales target and Anant Raj's data centre angle give the sector two differentiated stories running simultaneously.
  • NIFTY IT — +10 points of participation, but A/D was only 0.11 today — near-total decline within the sector despite the aggregate participation improving. This is likely a base effect: participation was so depressed it could only go up. Not a confirmed rotation.

Rotating out

  • NIFTY AUTO — −47 points of participation over five sessions, ranked 13th of 14 on the month. The steepest participation drop in the window by a wide margin. Strong IIP data (+7.3% manufacturing) has not translated into Auto sector buying.
  • NIFTY METAL — −27 points from the top-ranked 1-month position. Classic 'leadership fading': Metal leads by 1-month return but participation is draining. When the best-performing sector starts losing breadth, it often signals the momentum is maturing.
  • NIFTY HEALTHCARE — −25 points, ranked 7th. Healthcare is a defensive. Defensive participation contracting in a weak market usually means one thing: institutions are raising cash, not rotating to cyclicals.
  • NIFTY PHARMA — −20 points, ranked 4th on the month. Both defensives (Healthcare and Pharma) losing participation simultaneously is uncommon. When two unrelated sectors both shed breadth in the same direction, the common driver is usually fund flows, not sector news.
The rotation story today has two layers. On the surface: Banks in, Auto out. Underneath: two defensives (Healthcare, Pharma) shedding participation at speed. When defensives sell off in a weak large-cap market, it is often a cash-raising signal, not sector rotation. The sustainability of DII buying at ₹4,977 crore — absorbing 212% of FII selling — is the number that determines whether this is a floor or a pause before the next leg down.

GUIDE & METHODOLOGY

How to Read This Report

The order matters more than the sections. Most readers open at the index level and stop there — which is the habit that makes a narrow market look healthy.
Step 1

Start with participation, not the index

Open §7. Which direction is the number of stocks above their 20-day average moving? That is the market’s direction of travel, not the index close.

Step 2

Then check the index against it

If the Nifty rose but fewer stocks are above their 20-day average than yesterday, participation is falling inside a rising index. That divergence is the most important thing on the page.

Step 3

Read sector leadership across timeframes

In §6b, compare each sector’s 1-day move against its 1-week and 1-month bars. A sector strong on the day but weak on the month is a bounce. A sector strong on all three is a trend.

Step 4

Cross-check each sector&rsquo;s move against its own breadth

A sector that rose today but whose participation column is falling has a narrow move — a few stocks doing the work. §6b row next to §7 row is the comparison.

Step 5

Go straight to the flagged sectors

The pack has already joined breadth to the scanners for you. Read §7’s notable flags and §9’s scanner names together — that join is already done.

Step 6

Ask whether the money agrees with the positioning

Compare §11 (what institutions bought and sold) with §13 (where the derivatives positioning sits). Conviction shows when both point the same way.
